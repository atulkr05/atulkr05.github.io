Facial privacy protection research · 1999 to 2026

Every way researchers have tried to protect a face, in one catalogue. Blur and pixelation, differential privacy, generative de-identification, adversarial cloaks and makeup, unlearnable images, template protection and encrypted matching, soft-biometric privacy, training-data privacy, machine unlearning, and the attacks that broke many of them. Each entry lists venue, datasets, models, approach, findings, limitations and code.

About this atlas

Faceprint Atlas is a curated catalogue, not a systematic review. Inclusion favours work that introduced a technique, broke one, or shaped how the field evaluates itself. Venues and years were checked against arXiv metadata and proceedings; preprints may since have been published. Findings are summaries, so read the paper before citing a number. Code links point only to repositories that could be confirmed.

The same data is available as an Excel workbook with one tab per year and per family, and as a CSV of any filtered view from the library.
